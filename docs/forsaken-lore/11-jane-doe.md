## 1. Who Jane Doe is

The investigator. The only survivor who **chose** to be here.

Everyone else on this roster was taken — in their sleep, mid-accident, after death, by unknown means. Jane Doe **worked out where her husband had gone, and followed him in.**

> **[CANON]** "Jane Doe was the **loving wife of John Doe**, but after his disappearance she became **obsessive with finding out where her husband is**."
> — [Official Forsaken Wiki, *Jane Doe*](https://forsaken.wiki/Jane_Doe)

> **[CANON]** "Jane's personality is best described as **having her guard always up**, barely letting people truly into her life after the events of her past life. She's an **inquisitive person**, documenting the people and other things she has found within the realm. She has a **one-sided disgust for the admins for the neglect they have dealt**."

She is the game's **narrator-by-proxy**: the character who exists to write down what happened to everyone else. And she is furious.

### Real-world basis

Between **2004 and 2006**, Baszucki, Cassel and other early Roblox employees created dozens of alternate accounts for testing — **John and Jane Doe** among them.

In **early March 2017**, a rumour spread that the John Doe account would hack Roblox on **March 18th**. Roblox YouTubers — **ECKOSOLDIER** and **BigB** are named — amplified it with sensationalised videos. **Nothing happened.** Games added John Doe references as a joke, and on **21 March 2017** Roblox published a blog post explaining the accounts' origins and reassuring users.

Forsaken's move: take a moral panic about a test account and ask what it would have been like **to be the person it was actually about**.

## 2. Life before the Realm

### The job

> **[CANON]** "Jane Doe is a **retired tester who worked at ROBLOX**, but **left the company with a broken heart** after her husband went missing as **something grew out of the servers at Roblox HQ**."

She and John both worked under the admins. **John Doe was a Roblox employee working for the admins, joined by his wife Jane Doe.**

**[SECONDARY]** Both specialised in **fixing faulty code**.

Which makes what follows exquisitely cruel: **the couple whose job was fixing broken code were destroyed by broken code that nobody would let them fix.**

### The report that was ignored

Jane Doe is the person who found The Spectre.

> **[CANON]** "**Bug reporters such as Jane Doe** let Builderman and the other admins know about this bug, but **downplayed it saying that it would be fixed**."
> **[CANON]** "As the testers told Builderman about the issues, **he shrugged them off, saying the Admins had it under control**."

She did her job **exactly right**. She found the fault, escalated it, and kept escalating it. Builderman told her it was handled.

Then the thing she'd reported **took her husband.**

Her "one-sided disgust for the admins for the neglect they have dealt" is not a character quirk. **It is the correct response to what was done to her.** And "one-sided" is the sharpest word in her file: the admins have never acknowledged any of it. Builderman is in the same Lobby, calling people interns, rebuilding a cardboard robot. He does not appear to know what he did to her specifically.

### John

> **[CANON]** "John Doe was corrupted by the code and went missing."

Her husband was consumed by the exact defect she had filed a ticket about.

Her own words about him, as a Lobby NPC:

> **[CANON]** "Have you... have you seen him yet? **John, the one with the overgrown arm...** ... He **wasn't always that way**. John... **the real John**... he'd never hurt a fly. He was the **sweetest person you'd ever meet**..."

That is the emotional centre of Jane Doe's character. She has **found** him. He is in the Realm. He is a Killer with a deformed, overgrown arm, and she is reduced to stopping strangers and asking whether they've met him — then immediately explaining that the thing they met **isn't him**.

She is not looking for him anymore. She is **defending his memory to people who only know him as a monster.**

### "Mold Girl"

> **[CANON]** "After the disappearance of her husband, she and John would have the **March 18th rumors** come to existence, with her being said that **she is the reason John Doe disappeared** — being nicknamed **'mold girl'** after a **black substance started growing around the house**."

So, in order:

1. Her husband is consumed by a corruption she had reported.
2. A **black substance starts growing around her house** — the corruption is physically spreading through her home.
3. The internet decides **she** is responsible for his disappearance.
4. She is given a mocking nickname — **"mold girl"** — for living in a house that is visibly rotting around her.
5. She leaves the company **with a broken heart**.

She lost her husband, was blamed for it by strangers, and was mocked for the evidence of the thing that took him.

And the cruellest part, which the first version of this document missed:

> **[CANON]** **Jane blames herself for the faulty coding that took over John Doe.** Both of them worked on faulty code — Jane on focus missions.

So the strangers calling her responsible were not telling her anything she hadn't already decided. **She agrees with them.** She filed the report, she was ignored, and she has concluded that the thing she failed to get fixed is the thing that ate her husband.

That is the guilt The Spectre selected her for — and it means her contempt for the admins is not only anger at them. It is anger at the people who made her share the blame for something she tried to stop.

The black substance around the house was **the parasite itself**: the same corruption that leeched onto John, grew over his body, and tore his spine out of his back. It followed him home. She lived in it. See [`00-the-world-and-the-spectre.md`](00-the-world-and-the-spectre.md).

> **[CANON]** Her Level 100 achievement is named **"Mold Girl."**

The game turned the slur into a trophy. Read that as reclamation or as one more indignity — both readings work.

## 3. How she was Forsaken — the choice

The distinguishing fact of the character.

> **[CANON]** "Jane **researched and investigated the disappearance all by herself**, where she would stumble upon many things such as **the Spawn Cult**. Jane would figure out he was **taken to the realm of The Spectre**, **following it into the realm of Forsaken**."

Alone. Unsupported. Against an entity nobody would admit existed. And she **solved it** — and then, knowing exactly what was on the other side, **she went in after him.**

Note the incidental detail: her investigation **turned up the Spawn Cult** along the way. She independently uncovered a cannibalistic murder cult while looking for her husband, and it wasn't even the thing she was after.

**[SECONDARY]** Her NPC dialogue describes her as "a top researcher and investigator living in an unknown location who created a **board to connect the dots** and uncover the truth behind the John Doe incident." A literal conspiracy corkboard — which is presumably the ancestor of the **Lobby corkboard**.

### The scrapyard

> **[CANON]** "Upon entering, Jane found herself in a **'scrapyard' of sorts, serving as a waiting room**. Taking advantage of this, Jane **began documenting everything she could about the realm**. She would write **three files** pertaining to **Dusekkar, Shedletsky, and Builderman** while **going unnoticed**."

She arrives in hell and her first instinct is **to take notes.**

She was unnoticed in a holding area — she could have hidden, or looked for John, or panicked. Instead she started **building an intelligence file on the inhabitants**, and she picked her subjects deliberately: **the three admins.** The three people who ran the company that ignored her. She went straight for the people she blames.

### The confiscation

> **[CANON]** "Eventually, **The Spectre took away her documents and wedding ring, scattering them**."

Two objects, taken together, and the pairing is the point:

- **The documents** — her work, her method, her way of fighting back.
- **Her wedding ring** — John. The reason for all of it.

The Spectre took her **evidence and her marriage** and **scattered them** across the Realm. That is why the player has to go and collect folders to unlock her.

**Every Jane Doe quest is a player retrieving a widow's research and her wedding ring, piece by piece.**

## 4. The lore keeper

Jane Doe is not just a survivor — she is **how Forsaken tells its story.**

> **[CANON]** "Jane Doe serves as **the lore keeper** and resides in the **basement of the Lobby**, and also gives the player **quests** in order to collect **Documents** and unlock her as a playable character."

### The three quests

| Quest | Unlocks | Note |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **"Aim for the Top"** | **Shedletsky's** document | His ambition — the thing that destroyed him |
| **"Once Was No Longer Is"** | **Dusekkar's** document | What he was, he no longer is |
| **"Fundamental Principle of Inertia"** | **Builderman's** document | **A body at rest stays at rest.** She named the file about the man who refused to act after the law of *not moving*. |

Those titles are Jane Doe's character assessments, and they are savage. Especially Builderman's. She had one shot at summarising the administrator who ignored her reports, and she chose **inertia**.

> **[CANON]** "**Jane Doe is the first Survivor to be obtained through alternative means**, rather than being purchased in any shops."

You cannot buy her. You have to **do her research for her.**

## 5. In the Realm

> **[CANON]** "She now **tries her best to help the others, even those she dislikes**, as her research about the surroundings she's in continues."

**"Even those she dislikes."** Which includes, necessarily, the admins — Builderman especially. She helps him anyway. She keeps writing.

And she is keeping Shedletsky's secret:

> **[CANON / SECONDARY]** Jane Doe claims in her files that Shedletsky's **smiles and lighthearted demeanour feel forced** — and she **withholds this from the other Survivors.**

She has independently detected the biggest lie in the Realm, by observation alone, and she is sitting on it. That makes her the **second** person covering for Shedletsky, alongside Dusekkar, for entirely different reasons.

**[CANON]** She is also **a big music person, and plays many piano songs.**

That detail matters more than it looks. Everything else about Jane Doe is grief, work, and guardedness. The piano is the one thing she does that isn't investigation.

## 6. The kit as characterisation

**Sentinel** — combat and stunning.

> **[CANON]** "Her playstyle is centered around using her **Crystal Pitch** to build **status effects** on the Killer, and then **stun them and heal herself** with her **Hatchet**."

| Ability | Effect |
|:--|:--|
| **Crystal Pitch** | Builds status effects on the Killer over time |
| **Hatchet** | Stuns the Killer and **heals Jane** |

What this says:

1. **She's a Sentinel, not a Support.** She documents everything and helps people she dislikes — but her *kit* is for **fighting**. She is not here to be nursed or to nurse.
2. **Crystal Pitch is accumulation.** Her power **builds up over time** — you apply it, stack it, and it pays off later. That is her whole method: patient, cumulative evidence-gathering, and then the hit lands.
3. **"Pitch" is a music word.** For the character who plays piano. Her combat ability and her one private comfort share a vocabulary.
4. **The Hatchet heals her.** She is the survivor who **fixes herself by fighting back.** After years of being ignored, blamed, mocked and widowed, the mechanic that restores Jane Doe is **swinging at the monster.**

## 9. The story, assembled

> She and John both worked at Roblox HQ. They fixed broken code. They were married, and by every account they were happy, and he was the sweetest person you would ever meet.
>
> Then some old code in the servers started going wrong, and Jane — because it was literally her job — found it and reported it.
>
> Builderman said it would be fixed. She reported it again. He said the Admins had it under control. Everybody at HQ could see the cracks and he kept saying the same thing.
>
> And then the thing she had been filing tickets about **took her husband.** It got into him and corrupted him and he went missing.
>
> A black substance started growing around her house.
>
> That was the part the internet found funny. Rumours went around that John Doe was going to hack Roblox on the eighteenth of March; YouTubers made videos; none of it was true. And somewhere in the noise people decided **she** was the reason he'd disappeared, and started calling her *mold girl*, because of the rot spreading through the house where she used to live with him.
>
> She left the company with a broken heart, and then she did something nobody else in this story does.
>
> She investigated it herself. Alone. She built a board and connected the dots. She turned up a cannibal cult along the way and kept going, because that wasn't what she was looking for. And eventually she worked out exactly what had happened and exactly where he had been taken.
>
> And then, knowing precisely what was on the other side, she followed it in.
>
> She landed in a scrapyard that served as a waiting room, unnoticed. She could have hidden. She started taking notes instead — three files, on Dusekkar, on Shedletsky, and on Builderman. She named Builderman's file after the principle that a body at rest stays at rest.
>
> The Spectre found her eventually, and took two things: her documents, and her wedding ring. And it scattered them.
>
> So now she lives in a basement and asks passing strangers to bring her pieces of her own research back.
>
> She has found John. He's here. He has an overgrown arm and he kills people, and when she talks about him she can't finish a sentence without explaining that this isn't him, that the real John wouldn't hurt a fly.
>
> She helps everyone, including the admins she despises, because she has decided that's what you do. She's noticed Shedletsky is lying about something and she hasn't told anyone. She writes everything down.
>
> And sometimes she plays the piano.

## 10. Open questions

1. **Can John be saved?** She clearly believes there's a "real John" still in there. That's either the endgame or the cruellest thing in the game.
2. **Will she confront Builderman?** They're in the same Lobby. She wrote a document accusing him of inertia.
3. **What's in the full documents?** We have titles and fragments.
4. **Why does she hide Shedletsky's secret?** She owes him nothing. Something made her decide the group can't bear it.
5. **Did she ever get the ring back?** Documents are collectible. The ring is mentioned as scattered with them.
6. **Did she know she couldn't leave?** Willing entry is not the same as informed consent.
7. **What else did her investigation turn up?** She found the Spawn Cult by accident. There may be more.
