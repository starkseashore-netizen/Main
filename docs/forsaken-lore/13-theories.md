# 13 — Fan Theories, Graded by Evidence

> Forsaken has an enormous fandom and a correspondingly enormous volume of headcanon. This file contains **only theories with real supporting evidence**, each with the evidence laid out so you can judge it yourself.

---

## How theories are rated here

| Rating | Meaning |
|:--|:--|
| ★★★★★ | **Effectively confirmed.** Official material supports it directly; only the framing is interpretation. |
| ★★★★☆ | **Strong.** Multiple independent official details converge. Would not be surprising if confirmed tomorrow. |
| ★★★☆☆ | **Plausible.** Real evidence, but requires a leap. Could go either way. |
| ★★☆☆☆ | **Weak but popular.** Widely repeated; evidence is thin or circumstantial. |
| ★☆☆☆☆ | **Fandom folklore.** No real evidence. Listed because you will encounter it constantly. |

**Excluded from this file:** AUs, swap-AUs, ship content, "my headcanon lore," and the SEO content-farm articles that invent character names wholesale. See [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md) for why.

## Theory 1 — Most or all Survivors were dead before they arrived

**Rating: ★★★★☆ Strong**

### The claim

The Realm is an afterlife — purgatory, hell, or something between — and the Survivors did not get "kidnapped" so much as **die and go somewhere**.

### The evidence for

| Evidence | Source |
|:--|:--|
| **Guest 1337 definitively died.** He detonated a grenade at point-blank range, ignited an oil tank, killed the Bacon General. *"To the rest of the world, however, Guest 1337 was dead."* | **[CANON]** |
| **Chance was murdered** by iTrapped with a stolen Darkheart, and "was later taken into The Spectre's realm" | **[CANON]** |
| **Azure was killed** by Two Time, then "**resurrected by The Spectre**" as a corpse puppeteered by a hat | **[CANON]** |
| **Two Time's only canon skin, Nightshade**, is linked to "them dying before being forsaken by Spectre" | **[SECONDARY]** |
| **Noob's own character description** calls the Realm **"their purgatory"** | **[CANON]** |
| **007n7's and Elliot's** in-Realm framing is "forced to play games for what feels like eternity" | **[CANON]** |
| Community framing: "an afterlife between purgatory and hell that can be accessed through **death or a coma**" | **[THEORY]** |

### The evidence against

| Counter-evidence | Source |
|:--|:--|
| **Dusekkar was taken alive, asleep, with his entire house.** No death involved. | **[CANON]** |
| **Jane Doe walked in voluntarily** while alive, following her husband. | **[CANON]** |
| **Elliot was Forsaken during an accident** — "getting into an accident" is not the same as dying in one, and **his scooter came with him**, which reads as physical transport. | **[CANON]** |
| The wiki consistently uses the language of **kidnapping, taking, and pulling in** — not of dying. | **[CANON]** |
| **Helperbot and Veeronica are machines.** Robots don't die in the usual sense, and both were taken. | **[CANON]** |

### Assessment

The strongest version is wrong; a weaker version is probably right.

**Death is clearly a common route in — but it is not the only one.** Dusekkar and Jane Doe are hard counterexamples: one asleep with his house, one on foot and alive. And the objects that come along (the scooter, the house) suggest **physical relocation**, not soul transit.

What the evidence actually supports: **The Spectre takes people who are at their most vulnerable and most emotionally saturated.** Often that means at the moment of death. Sometimes it means asleep, or mid-accident, or walking in after a husband. The common factor is **anguish**, not death.

**Where it's headed:** the "coma" variant is interesting precisely because it splits the difference — Elliot's accident and Chance's "still slightly alive" community version both fit it. If the devs ever formalise this, expect something like *"the dying, the sleeping, and the grieving."*

## Theory 2 — The Spectre is Roblox's own dead code, and the Realm is a server

**Rating: ★★★★★ Effectively confirmed**

### The claim

The Spectre isn't a demon or a god. It's **a software fault that became self-aware**, and the Realm is a corrupted corner of Roblox infrastructure.

### The evidence

This one is barely a theory — the official wiki states most of it outright:

| Evidence | Source |
|:--|:--|
| "The Spectre is **a being made out of code**" | **[CANON]** |
| It "started out as **the corruption inside of the Roblox Headquarters's Servers**" | **[CANON]** |
| It grew "as it got **more control of the code and files of the servers**" | **[CANON]** |
| "Completely composed of **old, defunct Roblox code**" | **[SECONDARY]** |
| It **manages and manipulates the abilities** of Survivors and Killers, **nerfing and buffing** them | **[CANON]** |
| The **Necrobloxicon** grants "access to the **Lua code that the Roblox world runs on**" — establishing that code-level access is the setting's ultimate power | **[CANON]** |
| Builderman's skin **BSOD** (Blue Screen of Death) | **[CANON]** |
| The corruption physically manifested as **a black substance growing around Jane Doe's house** | **[CANON]** |

### Assessment

**Confirmed in substance.** The only genuinely interpretive part is the *implication*: if The Spectre balances abilities like a live-service developer, then **the game's own patch notes are in-fiction acts of cruelty**, and the Realm is a server that someone forgot to shut down.

That reading is the single best thing in Forsaken's worldbuilding, and it is fully supported.

**The remaining open question** is the timeline, not the nature. The Spectre originated in Roblox HQ's servers — but Tyler's torment predates the current cast by **at least 200 years**. Either The Spectre existed before HQ, or time in the Realm doesn't run at the same rate as outside. Neither has been addressed.

## Theory 3 — The Phosphorus symbol marks the Seven Deadly Sins

**Rating: ★★★☆☆ Plausible, with real structure behind it**

### The claim

Every character bearing the **Phosphorus symbol** in their design represents one of the **Seven Deadly Sins**, with **Phosphorus** himself as either Pride or all seven combined.

### The evidence

| Character | Assigned sin | Reasoning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Nosferatu** | **Gluttony** | A vampire — consumption as identity |
| **Azure** (full name given as **Azurewrath**) | **Wrath** | The name contains it; his hatred of Two Time clouds his judgement |
| **Guest 666** | **Envy** | Documented canon: she lost constantly, envy grew, she injected scripts to win |
| **Phosphorus** | **Pride**, or all combined | A **direct agent of The Spectre** |

Supporting facts:

- **Phosphorus is canon** and is canonically an agent of The Spectre — though note his entry sits on the **Potential characters** page, which the wiki **flags as outdated** and tells readers to take with a grain of salt. He is a **planned** Killer, not a released one.
- The symbol is documented as appearing on **Phosphorus, Azure and Nosferatu** specifically.
- **The symbol genuinely recurs** across character designs — that part is observable, not invented.
- **Guest 666 = Envy is airtight.** Her entire canon backstory is envy, start to finish.
- **"Azurewrath"** is a real weapon name (Diablo II), so the wrath reading is baked into the name itself.

### The problems

1. **Nobody has filled the other four.** Lust, Sloth, Greed and Pride have no confirmed holders. A seven-slot theory with three slots filled is a pattern-match waiting to be falsified.
2. **Azure's wrath is imposed, not intrinsic.** Canonically **he wants to forgive Two Time and The Spectre won't let him.** If his rage is a puppet-string rather than a sin, he's a poor fit.
3. **Chance is the obvious Greed candidate and isn't marked** — and he isn't greedy anyway; he threw money away deliberately.

### Assessment

**The symbol is real, the Guest 666 fit is perfect, and the framework is elegant.** But three of seven, with one of those three looking shaky on inspection, is exactly how coincidental patterns present themselves.

**Watch for:** a new Phosphorus-marked character who obviously maps to Lust, Sloth or Greed. One clean fourth data point would move this to ★★★★☆ immediately.

## Theory 4 — Elliot has a hidden connection to The Spectre

**Rating: ★★★☆☆ Plausible — the fandom's favourite, and genuinely seeded**

### The claim

Forsaken's kindest character has an undisclosed relationship with The Spectre — possibly as a former **messenger** who betrayed it and was cast down.

### The evidence for

| Evidence | Strength | Source |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Elliot's **Anniversary** skin has a **large "S" on the hat** that is **a confirmed reference to The Spectre** | **Strong** — official, and **no other Survivor has anything like it** | **[CANON]** |
| He has a skin literally named **Messenger**, with a **winged visor** | Moderate | **[CANON]** |
| **Milestone IV** projects **neon yellow Valkyrie wings** on his visor — wings recur | Moderate | **[CANON]** |
| **Game Theory's Arke reading**: Arke was the Greek messenger goddess who **betrayed Zeus, was stripped of her wings, and was cast into the underworld** | Moderate — the shape fits unnervingly well | **[THEORY]** |
| His documented abduction is oddly mundane (a scooter accident) for a character with this much iconography | Weak | — |

### The evidence against

| Counter-evidence |
|:--|
| **Every single piece of it is in cosmetics**, and the wiki explicitly labels skins as non-canonical "what-ifs." |
| Elliot's actual written lore is **completely mundane** — family, pizzeria, exploiters, accident. No hint of anything else. |
| **Valkyrie** is also the name of an **upcoming original Survivor**, so the wings may be a crossover nod, not a clue. |
| The elaborate community versions (soul-merging with a sadistic messenger, a duplicate Elliot, the "real" one imprisoned) have **no sourcing whatsoever**. |

### Assessment

**Something is being seeded. What it is has not been stated.**

The Anniversary "S" is the load-bearing fact and it is officially confirmed: a **Spectre reference, on a Survivor cosmetic, unique to Elliot.** Combine that with two independent wing motifs and a skin named *Messenger*, and it stops looking accidental.

But cosmetic iconography is **exactly** where developers plant flavour that never becomes plot, and Elliot's written lore is aggressively ordinary.

**The honest position:** the strongest *aesthetic* case in the game, and the weakest *narrative* one. Treat the elaborate versions — imprisoned real Elliot, merged souls — as fanfiction. Treat "the devs are hinting at something with Elliot" as a reasonable bet.

## Theory 5 — BrightEyes' fate is Telamon's "utterly selfish and deplorable act"

**Rating: ★★★☆☆ Plausible**

### The claim

The unnamed act that shattered Telamon and birthed 1x1x1x1 involves **BrightEyes' death or banning** — either he caused it, or his neglect allowed it.

### The evidence for

| Evidence | Source |
|:--|:--|
| The act is **deliberately unnamed** and described in unusually harsh terms for a playable hero | **[CANON]** |
| **BrightEyes and Dusekkar** are the two named people who watched Telamon's decline with worry — putting her right at the centre | **[CANON]** |
| The **Hacklord (1x1x1x1)** skin is a **"Heartbroken Shedletsky" who kills over the death/banning of his wife BrightEyes** | **[CANON — skin lore]** |
| BrightEyes has **her own skin and a dedicated wiki page**, so she is load-bearing, not background | **[CANON]** |
| Her fate is **never stated** anywhere in the main lore — a conspicuous omission for a named spouse | **[CANON]** |

### The evidence against

| Counter-evidence |
|:--|
| **Hacklord is a "what-if" skin.** If BrightEyes' loss already happened in the main timeline, an alternate-reality skin about it losing her makes less sense — it reads as *the branch where it did.* |
| The act is described as **"utterly selfish"** — a word that fits **neglect or abandonment** better than grief-driven revenge. |
| There is a simpler reading: Telamon did something selfish that **ruined her life**, and losing her is the *consequence*, not the act. |

### Assessment

The Hacklord skin cuts both ways and that's why this sits at three stars rather than four.

**What's solid:** BrightEyes is central to Telamon's fall, her fate is deliberately withheld, and losing her is a scenario the devs have explicitly modelled.

**What's speculative:** whether she is the *cause* or the *cost*.

**The likeliest shape**, reading "utterly selfish" literally: Telamon did something self-serving that destroyed her — and the horror of having caused it, plus the Necrobloxicon, is what split him. That makes 1x1x1x1 not just his hatred, but specifically **the part of him that did that to her**, which is why Shedletsky is so terrified of anyone finding out.

## Theory 6 — The Spectre tricked Builderman into building the door

**Rating: ★★☆☆☆ Weak but structurally interesting**

### The claim

Builderman didn't merely fail to stop The Spectre — he was **manipulated into building a portal into its realm**, in the manner of Maxwell and Wilson in *Don't Starve*.

### The evidence for

| Evidence |
|:--|
| Builderman is canonically **a builder** who made Robloxia brick by brick |
| He canonically **isolated himself** with the corruption, "denied every bit of the situation and wouldn't share more information with the other members of Roblox HQ" — i.e. he spent extended unsupervised time alone with it |
| The Spectre canonically **pulls locations, not just people** — which implies infrastructure |
| His guilt is framed as being about **"his mistakes"**, plural |

### The evidence against

| Counter-evidence |
|:--|
| **The official lore is unambiguous and sufficient**: he denied the problem, it grew, it took everyone. No portal required. |
| **No source mentions a portal.** This is imported wholesale from *Don't Starve*. |
| It arguably **weakens** the story — "tricked by a demon" is a much cheaper failure than "could not admit he was wrong." |

### Assessment

**Almost certainly not literal.** But it is worth listing because it identifies a real gap: canon says Builderman spent a long time **alone** with a growing intelligence, deliberately keeping everyone else out.

Whatever happened in that room is not documented. The theory is wrong about the portal and right about the gap.

## Theory 7 — The Realm is what's left of Robloxia

**Rating: ★★★☆☆ Plausible, and the most unsettling one here**

### The claim

The Spectre didn't carve a prison out of a living world. **It ate the world**, and the Realm is most of what remains.

### The evidence for

| Evidence | Source |
|:--|:--|
| The Spectre is "responsible for the **first successful Collapse of Robloxia, a mass genocide that supposedly caused the extinction of the Robloxian race**" | **[SECONDARY]** |
| It canonically **takes locations** out of Robloxia, not just people — the maps are stolen real places | **[CANON]** |
| It took **the admins one by one**, removing the world's moderation entirely | **[CANON]** |
| It is "**constantly growing the cast**, taking more and more people" | **[CANON]** |
| It has "**total control over Robloxia as a whole**" | **[SECONDARY]** |

### The evidence against

| Counter-evidence |
|:--|
| The "Collapse / extinction" claim comes from the **Villains Wiki**, not `forsaken.wiki`. It's the strongest possible version of the claim and the least officially grounded. |
| **People are still being reported missing** (Veeronica) — implying a functioning outside world with people in it to notice. |
| **Mafioso is explicitly *not* forsaken** and is still out there hunting Chance. |
| **iTrapped** left to free his friends from the Banlands; the Banlands presumably still exist. |

### Assessment

**The outside world is clearly still there** — Veeronica's missing report, Mafioso's continued hunt, and iTrapped's ongoing plan all require it.

But **"still there" and "intact" are different things**, and the direction of travel is unambiguous: The Spectre is *constantly growing the cast*, it takes places as well as people, and it has already removed an entire layer of governance.

The most defensible version: **Robloxia is not dead, but it is being consumed, and nobody left outside knows why.** The "extinction" framing is probably a later stage, or an overstatement — but the trend line supports it.

## Theory 8 — 007n7 took his own life

**Rating: ★★☆☆☆ Weak, popular, and worth handling carefully**

### The claim

After losing c00lkidd and failing to find him, 007n7 died by suicide, and that's how he was Forsaken.

### The evidence for

| Evidence |
|:--|
| His canon state is "**broken by his son's disappearance**" after an exhaustive, failed search |
| The official wiki says he was Forsaken by **"unknown means"** — leaving a gap |
| It fits the pattern of death-adjacent abductions (Guest 1337, Chance) |

### The evidence against

| Counter-evidence |
|:--|
| **The official wiki says "unknown means."** It does not say this. |
| His lore was written by **Soul**, who **acknowledged basing key parts of the characterisation on personal projection** — and the team has since committed to redoing him. Claims from that era are the *least* reliable in the game. |
| It is not required by anything. Plenty of survivors were taken alive. |

### Assessment

**Not canon, and this one deserves more care than the others.**

Given the documented history — a writer who acknowledged projecting personally onto this specific character, subsequently removed from the team, with the studio publicly committing to redo him — a heavy claim like this, unsupported by the wiki, should not be repeated as fact.

**What is canon:** his son vanished, he searched, he put up missing posters, he never found him, and something took him by unknown means. That is devastating on its own and doesn't need the addition.

## Theory 9 — The agent at Two Time's murder is Phosphorus

**Rating: ★★★★★ Effectively stated — this is documented, not theorised**

> **Correction.** An earlier version of this file rated this ★★★★☆ and called it "the best-supported open thread in Forsaken," treating the killer of Investigative Iris as a deliberately-withheld mystery. **That was wrong — the wiki names him.** I had found the pieces on separate pages and failed to put them together.

### What the pages actually say

The **Two Time** and **Azure** pages describe "an unknown individual" who threw Iris off the cliffside, and say that same individual transformed Aspen into **Azure**, a rotted corpse puppeteered by a hat The Spectre controls.

The **Phosphorus** entry closes the loop:

> **[CANON — on a page flagged outdated]** **Phosphorus** is a Killer **planned** for Forsaken and **an agent of The Spectre**. Very little is known about him. His design carries **a symbol also present on both Azure and Nosferatu**, and **Phosphorus was responsible for Investigative Iris's death during her investigation into the Spawn Cult**, leading to her being Forsaken.

### The caveats that keep this from being flatly settled

1. **Phosphorus is a *planned* Killer**, not a released one.
2. The entry sits on the **Potential characters** page, which the wiki **explicitly flags as outdated**, noting it came from a dev team member, that the story "has changed many times," and to take it **with a grain of salt**.
3. The two accounts are **not identical in scope.** Two Time's page credits the individual with **both** Iris's death **and** Aspen's transformation into Azure. The Phosphorus entry credits him only with **Iris's death**. The **shared symbol on Azure's design** is what ties him to the second half.

### Assessment

**The identity is documented; the reliability of the document is the open question**, which is a very different thing from a mystery.

What it implies is still the important part: The Spectre **does not only collect victims — it dispatches agents into the world**. Phosphorus was at the Spawn Cult, in the field, killing a witness to an investigation.

Which reframes Two Time's entire story. A cult whose doctrine specified a murder in a particular nightshade field, an elder who intervened at exactly the moment they tried to leave, and an operative standing by to silence the witness and collect the body. The question stops being *why did Two Time do it* and becomes **how much of it was arranged around them.**

Note also the symbol: it appears on **Phosphorus, Azure and Nosferatu**. That is the same marking the Seven Deadly Sins theory (Theory 3) is built on — so these two theories are reading the same evidence, and a resolution to one moves the other.

## Theory 10 — Tyler is still out there

**Rating: ★★☆☆☆ Weak, but the payoff would be enormous**

### The claim

Tyler — The Spectre's first victim, made formless and forced to watch — still exists, and will matter.

### The evidence for

| Evidence | Source |
|:--|:--|
| Tyler is canon, is the **first victim**, and **named The Spectre** | **[CANON]** |
| He was made **formless, exactly like The Spectre itself** | **[CANON]** |
| He was made to **observe everything that happens** — meaning he is a **witness to the entire history of the Realm** | **[CANON]** |
| **Naming something** is a position of power in most fiction, and the wiki makes a point of it | — |
| TV Tropes' WMG records theories that he is still present, and even that Elliot is his messenger | **[THEORY]** |

### The evidence against

| Counter-evidence |
|:--|
| **[THEORY]** The WMG page also holds that The Spectre eventually **cast him into a void to cease existing** after ~200 years — which would close the thread entirely. |
| He has no in-game presence at all: no skin, no NPC, no voiceline. |

### Assessment

**Currently just a name in a backstory.** But look at what canon actually gives him:

- He is **formless, like The Spectre** — the only other entity in that category.
- He has **watched everything** since the beginning.
- He **named** the thing that did it to him.
- He **beat it once already** by going numb, which is the only documented instance of anyone frustrating The Spectre.

That is a *lot* of setup for a character who exists in two paragraphs. Whether the payoff ever comes is anyone's guess — but if Forsaken ever wants a way out for its cast, the prisoner who stopped being afraid and gave the monster its name is the most thematically correct answer available.

## Theories deliberately excluded

Encountered constantly; not worth listing above.

| Excluded | Why |
|:--|:--|
| **Any "AU" or "swap AU" lore** | Explicitly fan-authored. The wiki's `Swapsaken/` pages document an official alternate **game mode**, not alternate lore. |
| **Ship-based backstories** | Fanfiction, with the notable exception of Two Time/Azure, who **were canonically dating**. |
| **Character-name lists from SEO sites** | "Veeronica, Demo, Host, Invicible, Mike, Posty, Conflictor, Swiftshift" and similar — **mostly fabricated**. Veeronica is real; most of the rest are not. See [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md). |
| **"X is secretly the Spectre"** (for most values of X) | Contradicts canon: The Spectre is bodiless, made of code, and originated in HQ's servers. Elliot's version is listed above only because it has cosmetic evidence. |
| **Milestone-skin narratives treated as canon** | Explicitly "what-if" alternate realities — with the documented exceptions of Two Time's **Nightshade** and c00lkidd's **Mafioso**. |
