## 1. Who Builderman is

The man in charge. And the single most important thing to understand about Forsaken's lore is that **the apocalypse is his fault** — not through malice, but through **denial**.

> **[CANON]** "Builderman is a **laid back Robloxian with a southern accent** who tries his best to show care for the **ROBLOX interns and other Survivors**, doing what he can to **protect them due to his mistakes**."
> — [Official Forsaken Wiki, *Builderman*](https://forsaken.wiki/Builderman)

**"Due to his mistakes."** The wiki says it in his one-line personality summary. His entire present-day behaviour is framed as **restitution**.

He is not a villain. He is something rarer in games: **a decent, well-liked man who caused a catastrophe by being unable to admit a problem was real.**

### Real-world basis

Based on **David Baszucki** — Roblox's founder and CEO — and the **@builderman** account. Forsaken takes the platform's founder and asks the uncomfortable question: *what if the guy running the place ignored the warnings?*

## 2. Life before the Realm

### The builder

> **[CANON]** "Builderman was **one of the first Robloxians alongside Dusekkar**, with Builderman going on to **lead Roblox HQ**. He and a close friend of his would contribute to helping build modern day Robloxia, **brick by brick, exactly how they wanted it**."

He didn't inherit the world. **He made it.** With Dusekkar, by hand, the way they wanted it. That's why the fall lands as hard as it does — he is watching his own creation be eaten.

> **[CANON]** "He worked alongside **Shedletsky, Doombringer, and John and Jane Doe** and had **large ambitions**."

The full HQ roster. Note how many of them are now in the Realm: Shedletsky, Dusekkar, Jane Doe — and **John Doe is a Killer.**

**Almost everyone Builderman ever worked with is now damned, on both sides of the game.**

### The administrator

> **[CANON]** "Builderman was the **main leader within the Roblox Headquarters**, leading **Admins and other interns** to keep helping and maintaining systems in Robloxia."

"Interns" matters. He wasn't running a faceless bureaucracy — there were **young people** working under him, and he mentions them first in his personality description. He still calls them that. He is still, in some corner of his head, their boss.

## 3. The failure

This is the centre of Forsaken, and it deserves to be spelled out step by step.

### Step 1 — The corruption appears

> **[CANON]** "Disaster struck when **The Spectre started out as the corruption inside of the Roblox Headquarters's Servers**."

It starts small. A fault in old, defunct code.

### Step 2 — The testers report it

> **[CANON]** "Bug reporters such as **Jane Doe** let Builderman and the other admins know about this bug, but **downplayed it saying that it would be fixed**."

**The system worked.** QA found it. QA escalated it. **Jane Doe did her job.**

### Step 3 — He handles it alone

> **[CANON]** "When the old code started to corrupt, **he tried to deal with it alone. He denied every bit of the situation and wouldn't share more information with the other members of Roblox HQ.**"

This is the decisive failure, and it is a **failure of leadership, not competence**:

- He **denied** it.
- He **isolated** the information.
- He **cut out his own team** — a team that included a wizard, a legendary swordsman, and the people who found the bug in the first place.

### Step 4 — It grows

> **[CANON]** "Spectre **grew and became stronger and more powerful** as it got **more control of the code and files of the servers**."

### Step 5 — Everyone can see it

> **[CANON]** "As the testers told Builderman about the issues, **he shrugged them off, saying the Admins had it under control**, all despite **everyone working there seeing the large cracks**."

Not one warning. **Ongoing, repeated warnings**, from staff who could all see the damage, met with a line of corporate reassurance.

*"The Admins had it under control."* It is the most damning sentence in Forsaken's lore.

### Step 6 — It starts taking people

> **[CANON]** "**Before the Roblox Army Vs. Bacon Empire war**, Spectre would start to **kidnap people and take places in Robloxia, including the admins themselves one by one.**"

And then the dominoes:

- The admins vanish → moderation collapses.
- Moderation collapses → the **Bacon Empire declares war on Guests** → **Guest 1337's** parents, people, war and death.
- Moderation collapses → unmoderated experiences rot → **Taph** goes vigilante → **sentient NPCs die**.
- Moderation collapses → exploiters run unchecked.

**Builderman's denial is the load-bearing beam of the entire setting.** Pull it out and most of the roster never gets hurt.

## 4. How Builderman was Forsaken

> **[CANON]** "At an unknown point, he would be **Forsaken by the Spectre**."

Unspecified — but with a grim logic to it. He was taken **along with the admins he failed to warn**, by the thing he insisted wasn't a problem.

## 5. In the Realm

Builderman is doing the only thing left: **trying to keep people alive, one round at a time.**

### Helperbot

The detail that defines his present-day existence.

> **[CANON]** "**Helperbot is a robot made to assist and inspire ROBLOX creators.** After their arrival within The Spectre's realm, they were **nicknamed as D3functB0t**, before being **slowly repaired by Builderman using parts of generators**."

Consider what this actually is:

- A machine built **to assist and inspire creators** — the mission statement of the platform he founded.
- Dragged into hell and renamed **"D3functB0t."** *Defunct.* Obsolete. Broken.
- Being **slowly repaired**, by hand, by the man who built the world it came from.
- **Out of parts stripped from the generators** — the same generators Survivors have to repair each round to survive.

He is cannibalising the machinery of his own torment to rebuild one small broken thing that used to help people.

It is not going to save anyone. It will not get him out. It is the purest possible expression of a man who cannot fix what he broke, fixing something.

**Helperbot's description**: a slim Robloxian frame of flat iron, exterior of **cheap material — cardboard and aluminium foil** — with a **cardboard box head, a robot face drawn on in black sharpie**, and three antennae. Builderman is rebuilding it out of rubbish, badly, and clearly with affection.

## 6. The kit as characterisation

**Support** — and, uniquely, he supports by **building**.

| Ability | Effect |
|:--|:--|
| **Sentry** | Places a turret that **slows the Killer**, protecting whoever's being chased |
| **Dispenser** | Constructs a **healing station** for Survivors in need |

Both are lifted from *Team Fortress 2*'s Engineer, and both are **exactly right** for him:

1. **He builds.** He built Robloxia brick by brick; now he builds sentries and dispensers brick by brick. Same man, infinitely smaller scope.
2. **He is infrastructure.** He doesn't fight, doesn't run, doesn't heal by hand. He **puts systems in place and hopes they hold** — which is precisely what he failed to do at HQ.
3. **His whole kit is protection he didn't provide when it counted.** A device that slows the threat. A device that heals the damage. Twenty years too late, at a scale of about eight people.

He is running a very small, very late, very sincere version of the job he botched.

## 7. Skins and variants

**Builderman/BSOD** — *Blue Screen of Death*. For a character whose entire failure was a software fault he refused to acknowledge, naming a skin after the moment a system admits it has crashed is not subtle, and it doesn't need to be.

## 8. The story, assembled

> He was there at the very start — him and Dusekkar, two of the first, laying Robloxia out brick by brick exactly how they wanted it. Then he took charge of the place: Roblox HQ, the admins, the interns, the systems that kept a whole world running.
>
> He was good company. Laid back, southern, liked by people. He had enormous ambitions and a team that could match them — Shedletsky, Dusekkar, Doombringer, John and Jane Doe.
>
> Then some old code started going wrong in the servers.
>
> His bug testers found it and told him. Jane Doe told him. He said it would be fixed.
>
> And then he did the thing that ended the world: he decided to handle it himself. He denied it was serious and stopped telling the rest of HQ what he knew. He had a wizard on staff and a legendary swordsman and the people who'd found the fault, and he locked all of them out of it.
>
> Meanwhile the thing in the servers got bigger. It took control of the code, then the files, then more. Everyone in the building could see the cracks, and they kept coming to him, and he kept saying the same thing: the Admins have it under control.
>
> They did not have it under control.
>
> It started taking people. And places. And then the admins themselves, one at a time, until there was nobody left to moderate anything.
>
> And with the admins gone, the Bacon Empire declared war on the Guests, and a boy called Guest 1337 lost his parents and then his people and then his life. And with the admins gone, unmoderated games rotted, and someone who'd idolised Builderman decided to clean them up personally, and sentient NPCs died in the explosions. And the exploiters did whatever they liked.
>
> All of it downstream of one man who could not say *I don't know what this is and I need help.*
>
> Eventually it took him too.
>
> Now he's here, in the wreckage, with the people he failed. Jane Doe is here — the one who filed the report — and she has a quiet contempt for the admins that she has earned. Taph is here, whose whole ruined life was an attempt to be him. John Doe is here, wearing something that used to be a colleague, hunting them all.
>
> Builderman sets up a turret to slow the monster down. He builds a dispenser so the wounded can patch themselves up. He calls them interns.
>
> And in between rounds he sits with a broken robot that was built to help creators and now gets called *Defunct*, and he repairs it a piece at a time with scrap he's pulled out of the generators, and its head is a cardboard box with a face drawn on in marker.
>
> He is never going to finish. He is never getting out. He is never going to be forgiven, partly because most of them don't know there's anything to forgive.
>
> He fixes it anyway.

## 9. Open questions

1. **How was he Forsaken?** "At an unknown point."
2. **Does he know what he caused?** He acts "due to his mistakes" — so he knows *something*. Whether he's connected the dots to the Bacon War and Taph is unclear.
3. **Has he ever spoken to Taph?** The most glaring gap in the game's relationship map.
4. **Does Jane Doe confront him?** She has documented disgust for the admins and is the lore keeper. They are in the same Lobby.
5. **Does he know about John Doe?** A former colleague is now a Killer.
6. **Why was he taken so late?** The admins went "one by one" and his abduction is separate and unspecified. Was he last?
7. **What is Helperbot for?** Nothing mechanical is documented. It may just be the point.
